# Tips for ADHD and autistic people

Homedial was designed with ADHD and autistic people in mind. This page explains the choices we made and
gives practical tips for setting up your home around them.

These are practical tips about using your smart home, not medical advice, and they don't diagnose or
treat any condition. For health questions, talk to a qualified professional.

## How Homedial is built

- **One number to set.** You pick one target temperature. Homedial works out the rest from your rooms.
- **Plain words.** The Reason sensor says what is happening in a sentence, so you don't have to read
  graphs or work out why the heat came on.
- **Slow, steady changes.** Homedial changes the thermostat in small steps, to help avoid big swings
  between too hot and too cold.
- **Fewer decisions.** With the extras, Auto picks Cool or Heat for you and waits before switching, so
  it doesn't flip back and forth.
- **Calm screens.** The Directive Labs themes in Homedial Backdrop use normal capitalization, no italics, no
  text you need to read under 15 px, and strong contrast. Calm is the lowest-stimulation look.

## Tips

1. **Start with one page.** Put the thermostat card, the Reason sensor and an Off button on one
   dashboard page. Add more only when you miss something.
2. **Pick Calm or Light, then leave it.** Choose one theme in Homedial Backdrop and keep it. A look that never
   changes is easier to read at a glance. Seasons, Holidays and Travel change the look on their own;
   skip them if surprises bother you.
3. **Let Auto decide.** If your thermostat can heat and cool, turn on Auto and set only the target.
   That is one less choice each day.
4. **Read the Reason, not the numbers.** When the house feels wrong, check the Reason sensor first. It
   says whether Homedial is waiting, stepping toward your target, or protecting a room.
5. **Use a steady routine.** Pick one daytime target and one night target and set them at the same
   times each day with a Home Assistant automation or schedule, instead of changing them by hand.
6. **Use a fan timer for a quick air boost.** With the extras, a 15- or 30-minute fan run moves air
   without changing your target, then stops by itself.
7. **Keep alerts for real problems.** The safety alert shows only when a guard trips. Turn off any
   other dashboard alerts you don't act on.
